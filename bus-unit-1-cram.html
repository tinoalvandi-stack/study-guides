Definition of business

A for-profit or non-profit organization involved in commercial, industrial, or professional commerce.

  • Commercial — pursuit of a profit margin (retail sale, bank loan, lawn service)
  • Industrial — anything manufactured (cars, clothing, software)
  • Professional — customized, knowledge-based services for a fee (lawyer, accountant, tutor)

One-liner to reuse: business is about people managing business functions.

Management

Plans, organizes, staffs, controls, leads people, processes and assets to reach a goal the best way possible. Answer five, even though one graphic shows four.

  • Planning maps the course and the resources needed
  • Staffing = recruit, interview, hire, orient
  • Controlling checks results against the goal (KPIs) and corrects
  • Leading = communicate, motivate, guide, coach, review — tied to culture

Managers manage people, processes and assets, and all six functions.

Levels and chain of command

Chain of command = who reports to whom. Front-line → supervisory → middle → officer/executive.

School: president/principal → deans and chairs → teachers and moderators → students. Apple: CEO/CFO → regional and store managers → shift leads → floor.

Strategy and HR build the org chart together.

Effective manager skills

  • Problem-solving — trade-offs with limited resources (executives lean here)
  • People skills — empathize while still influencing
  • Procedural — learn and run methods fast (supervisors lean here)
  • Leadership — inspire and motivate (glossary swaps in emotional intelligence; know both)

57% of employees have left a job because of a manager or leader (2019). People leave managers, not companies.

Manager vs leader

Management is doing things right; leadership is doing the right things. Leaders initiate the plan, managers execute it — not clean, since each does both. The split may be about growth.

Leadership inspires, motivates, influences and maximizes effort toward a common purpose.

HR: the 12 responsibilities, in order

Recruiting, staffing, legal issues, job descriptions, orientation and onboarding, training and professional development, performance management, compensation, company culture, employee terminations, exit interviews, employee benefits.

Orientation = one-time event, meet the company and coworkers. Onboarding = months-long process, learn culture, mission, systems, expectations.

Interviews and terminations need the most legal knowledge.

EEOA and the right people

Blocked in an interview: age, country of origin, marital status, sex. Work history and job performance are fair game.

The right people = interests, skills, experience matched to the role, so the employee is productive and fulfilled.

Good to Great, Jim Collins: first who, then what. Right people on the bus, in the right seats, before deciding where to drive.

The 6 employee benefits (VIP)

  • Health — medical, dental, vision
  • Retirement — 401k, stock options
  • PTO plus sick time
  • Life insurance plus long/short term disability
  • Maternity and paternity, bereavement, mental health
  • Free childcare or reimbursement

Professional development sits next to it: school reimbursement, continuing education, paid training, seminars.

Best places to work checklist: company culture, company values, compensation, employee benefits, professional development.

Strategy

A route or plan of action to achieve goals and objectives, informed by mission and vision. It says what to do and, more importantly, what to say no to.

Business strategy → the company earns a profit. Marketing strategy → the products earn a profit (target market, 4Ps).

Quiz pairings: with HR it sets who reports to whom; with information management, that the right data is mined; with operations, that tactics align to goals; with finance, capital structure and debt.

Mission / vision / goal / objective / tactic

Missionthe why you exist and the what you do
Visionthe where you end up if the mission works
Goalmeasurable end result, long term, 3–5 years
Objectivespecific, with available resources, usually 1 year
Tacticthe specific action that supports the strategy

Student example: goal = graduate in four years; objectives = keep a 3.0 and finish AP courses.

Missions and visions to have ready

Habitat for Humanity — mission: putting God’s love into action, bringing people together to build homes, communities and hope. Vision: a world where everyone has a decent place to live.

IKEA — mission: well-designed, functional home furnishings at prices so low as many people as possible can afford them. Vision: a better everyday life for the many people.

This school — mission: teach the Gospel of Jesus Christ, prepare students for higher education, build a community where service is valued and celebrated. Vision: a national leader in college preparatory education in an environment distinguished by Catholic values.

The 5 stages of the entrepreneurial process

Discovery → business plan → resourcing → run business → harvesting.

Find the idea, write it down, gather money/people/equipment, operate it, then cash out or sell.

On the board but not on the review sheet, so expect a question.

Traps that decide points

  • Accounting vs finance — accounting records, maintains and reports (looks back); finance manages assets, liabilities, cash flow, ROI/ROE and plans growth (looks forward). Asked back to back.
  • Advertising and customer service are not business functions — they sit under marketing’s eight.
  • Recruiting vs staffing — recruiting is the searching part; staffing is recruit, interview, hire, orient.
  • Orientation vs onboarding — one morning vs three months.
  • Which three compile info about the target market — strategy, information management, marketing. Not operations.
  • Software counts as industrial, not professional.
  • Formats to expect: multiple response, select THREE, select TWO, complete the outline, complete the sentence.
