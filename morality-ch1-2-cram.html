anatomy of a moral act · three elements, weigh all three

what will you do
moral object
the act itself. the decisive element in morality.
why
intention
subjective dimension, up to you. a bad intention contaminates a good act (helping someone across the street with a bad intention).
who · what · when · where · how
circumstances
a modifier. can increase, diminish, or shift responsibility.
can it make an evil act good? object: no. intention: no. circumstances: no. adultery and murder stay evil. all three must be good for the act to be good.

prudence, per aquinas

right reason in action.

the moral virtue that inclines you to discern a good, ethical, and moral life and to choose the means to accomplish it. the virtue that helps make the right choice.

discernment is the process of thinking what to do. prudence is the virtue that makes you good at it.

always wrong

perjury · rape · blasphemy · murder

no intention, no circumstance can justify them. abortion is named with murder: the deliberate killing of unborn human life by medical or surgical procedures.

created in the image of god

created out of love, in his image, and good. creation was good.

  • intellect
  • free will
  • capacity for love
  • inherent dignity
  • relationship and community

we are both body and soul. humans are the only creatures that know that they know.

conscience

because you are made in god's image and likeness, body and soul, you can hear the voice of god urging you to do good and avoid evil.

you know god's law through your conscience and fulfill it through love of god and neighbor. sin is a fault against reason, truth, and right conscience.

the ultimate question

what is the meaning of life? my purpose.

full set: what am i here for, why do i exist, what happens after i die, what is the meaning of life. it comes from the soul, which will not settle for a merely physical life.

augustine: our hearts are restless until they rest in god.

prayer, defined

conversation with god. the living relationship of the children of god with the father, son, and holy spirit. communion, sharing in the presence of god.

two parts: talking and listening. to listen, slow down and seek silence.

meditation = focus the mind on what god is saying. contemplation = mental prayer. jesus said pray together, with faith, with childlike simplicity, without becoming weary. our father = summary of the whole gospel.

salvation

the forgiveness of sins and restoration of friendship with god, which can be done by god alone.

humanity was and remains in need of a savior. jesus christ is god incarnate. paschal mystery = his passion, death, resurrection, ascension.

respect

for god: worship him alone. for creation: be a good steward; damaging it stops it proclaiming god's glory. for your body: creation is sacred and that includes you. for others: everyone has god-given dignity.

dignity is inherent (essential to being human), inviolable (no one may violate it), inalienable (cannot be taken away, even by your own sin).

male and female · complementarity

complementary = things that combine to bring out the best in one another. male and female complete each other.

neither is superior. only through the committed union of husband and wife in marriage can procreation and education of children happen as god intends.

most likely

stop

  • search out the facts. take out the emotion.
  • think about alternatives and consequences. imagine living with each choice.
  • others can help: parents, friends, mentors, coaches, the magisterium, penance.
  • pray for guidance.

means = the course of action or how something is done. good intentions cannot justify evil means. stealing a car to get someone to the hospital is still wrong.

cardinal virtues · the four pivotal virtues

  • prudence: right reason in action. knowing and choosing the right thing.
  • justice: giving god and each person his or her due by right. the respect they deserve.
  • fortitude: courage to persist in living a christian life. courage and perseverance.
  • temperance: controlling desires for physical pleasures. controlling wants.

theological virtues · habit · virtue

faith = belief in and personal knowledge of god. hope = trust in god's salvation. charity = love of god and neighbor. love = choosing good for others, even to personal sacrifice.

habit = something that becomes second nature. both good and bad actions are habit forming. virtue = a habitual and firm disposition to do the good.

sow an act, reap a habit; sow a habit, reap a character; sow a character, reap a destiny.

original holiness vs original justice

original holinessdirect relationship with god. shared in the divine life.
original justicedirect relationship with all creation. harmony between the first couple and all creation.

the pride of adam and eve led to their downfall and expulsion from eden. original sin = choosing their own will over god's. concupiscence = disordered desires that remain after baptism.

christianity vs nihilism

christianitycreated with intention. life has meaning.
nihilismno ultimate meaning, no purpose. denies any meaning in existence or religious beliefs.

solidarity · common good · subsidiarity

solidarity: the christian virtue of social charity and friendship. we are responsible for each other.

common good: the sum total of social conditions that allow people, as groups or individuals, to reach their fulfillment more fully and more easily.

subsidiarity: handled at the smallest level. a higher unit should not do what a lower unit can do as well or better.

the church

magisterium: the bishops in union with the pope, the living and teaching office of the church. (pope + bishops + teaching office.)

infallibility: gift of the holy spirit whereby the pope and bishops in union with him can definitively proclaim a doctrine of faith or morals for the belief of the faithful.

penance: for catholics who have fallen into grave sin since baptism. confess mortal sins at least once a year or before communion. sponsor: one catholic sponsor for the sacraments of initiation.

sin

an offense against god as well as a fault against reason, truth, and right conscience. basic cause: love for self over love for god.

sin was most intense at christ's passion, as witnessed by the hatred of jesus' enemies for the son of god.

soul · angels · body

soul: the innermost spiritual principle of human beings. with the body it forms one human nature. created immediately by god, immortal.

angels: spiritual creatures with intelligence and will who surpass humans in perfection.

theology of the body: john paul ii's integrated vision of the person: body, soul, spirit. he called the body a sacrament, from the greek mysterion, mystery.
